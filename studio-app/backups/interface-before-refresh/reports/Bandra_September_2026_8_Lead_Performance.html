Supreme HQ, Bandra — where the members come from

7,822 people have enquired at Bandra since the studio began recording leads. 1,006 became members, 1,439 are still members today, and 2,120 are still marked open. This report reads the whole file to answer three questions: which channels produce members rather than enquiries, how fast a lead has to be handled to convert, and what the studio is currently doing with the 2,120 people who have not said no.

Start here

The studio has a volume problem in its largest channel and a follow-through problem everywhere else. The trial is the gate, and the same day is the deadline.

Read the whole file and three things stand out above everything else.

First, the trial decides everything. 3,493 of 7,822 leads completed a trial; 1006 of them bought. Of the 4,329 who never took a trial, 95 ever bought anything. The trial is not a step in the funnel — it is the funnel.

Second, the sale happens the day the enquiry arrives. 61% of every membership Bandra sells is sold on the same day, and the median lead buys on the day it enquires. Everything after that is a small, slow tail.

Third, the studio’s biggest channel is its weakest. Website and online sources deliver 3,714 leads — 47% of the file — and convert 8.9% of them at ₹4,120 of lifetime value. Calls, messages and referrals together deliver 1,920 leads and convert 23.9% of them at ₹9,031.

The single sentence version

This is not a studio that cannot attract people. It is a studio that attracts 7,822 of them, converts the ones who walk in or ring up, and then leaves 2,120 of the rest sitting in a file whose average entry is 603 days old.

The trial is the gate

A lead who has taken a class converts at 12 times the rate of a lead who has not. Nothing else in the file is close.

3,493 leads completed a trial and 26.1% of them became members. Of the 4,329 who did not, 2.19% converted — 95 people out of 4,329. Mean lifetime value is ₹11,888 against ₹24 — effectively nothing.

This single fact reorganises everything else in this report. Cost per lead, source quality and channel mix all matter, but they matter because they change how many trials get booked. A channel that produces a hundred leads and twenty trials is worth more than one that produces three hundred leads and twenty trials, and the studio currently reports only the first number.

Speed decides it

61% of wins happen on day zero. The studio is a same-day counter with an unmeasured response time.

Of the 951 Bandra leads that converted and carry a usable date pair, 577 — 61% — bought on the same day they enquired. The next day accounts for 6.3%, and the whole of the first week for 74.3%. The median lead buys on the day it enquires.

The tail is small but instructive. The 194 wins that took a month or longer are worth ₹42,525 on average, against ₹38,832 for same-day wins. Patience is rewarded, but only 20.4% of the studio’s business comes that way. The practical conclusion is not that slow leads are valuable; it is that a lead who has not converted within the first week is almost certainly gone.

What this means for the front desk

Every enquiry that arrives and is answered the next morning has already lost most of its value. The studio’s conversion rate is therefore a description of how fast it answers, not of how persuasive it is — and unlike persuasion, answering speed is entirely within its control.

Where the leads come from

Volume and value sit in different channels, and the gap between them is the studio’s largest untapped gain.

Bandra records six channels. Website & online is dominant at 3,714 leads — 47% of the file — and converts at 8.9%. Calls & messages brings 881 leads at 25.3%, and Referrals & word of mouth brings 1,039 at 22.7%.

The named sources inside those channels sharpen the picture further. Website is the largest source in the file at 3,090 leads and the weakest of the major sources at 8.1%. Incoming sms is the strongest at 32.0% on 50 leads, and Incoming call carries the highest lifetime value at ₹20,201. Website - AC is the weakest line in the file on every measure — 45 leads, 2% trial completion, 0.0% conversion and ₹0 of value.

What leads ask for

The studio records the class every lead wants. It is the most complete demand signal they hold, and it is not used.

Class preference is recorded on almost every Bandra lead. Barre 57 is named on 5,508 leads, PowerCycle on 2,014 — between them 96% of the entire file. They convert at almost identical rates (13.2% and 12.7%).

Because the preference is stated at the point of enquiry, it is the cheapest personalisation the studio could act on: the trial should be booked into the class the lead asked for, at the time they asked for it, wherever that is achievable. That is a scheduling decision rather than a marketing spend.

The file the studio is not working

2,120 open leads. A mean age of 603 days. 1,836 of them more than six months old.

The open file is larger than every membership the studio has ever sold at Bandra. It is also, on the evidence of its own timestamps, almost entirely historical: 56 of the 2,120 open leads are less than thirty days old, and 1,836 have been open for more than six months. 1,076 sit at the same stage — pricing and schedule shared, no decision ever recorded.

That is not a pipeline. It is a filing cabinet, and it is distorting every conversion figure the studio reports, because a lead that was quietly lost two years ago is still counted as in play. The honest disposition of this file is the least glamorous and most valuable piece of work on this page: it costs a week and it makes every subsequent number trustworthy.

Lead flow through 2026

Trial completion has risen sharply this year. Conversion has not followed it, and the volume is falling.

Read as a cohort — every lead grouped by the month it arrived — the 2026 file shows a studio getting better at the middle of its funnel and no better at the end of it. Trial completion has moved from 37.9% in January to 54.5% in September, a rise of 17 points. Conversion has moved from 9.2% to 6.3%, down 3 points.

Volume tells the second half of the story. Bandra created 189 leads in September against 135 in the same month last year, and the 2026 line has run below 2025 from March onwards.

Put together: fewer people are enquiring, more of those who do are getting into a class, and the same proportion are buying. The studio has fixed the part of the funnel it can see and is losing the part it cannot — the top.

Recommendations

Twelve actions, ordered by the value each releases
01

Nothing matters more than the trial — treat it as the only target

Studio Manager + CRM

Reasoning3,493 of 7,822 Bandra leads completed a trial and 1006 of them became members. Of the 4,329 who never took a trial, 95 ever bought anything. A completed trial converts at 26.1%; a lead without one converts at 2.19%. Mean lifetime value is ₹11,888 against ₹24.

Expected impactEvery hour spent on a lead who has not booked a class is worth a fraction of the same hour spent getting that class booked. The studio’s single operational target should be the number of trials booked this week, and every other lead metric should be read as a consequence of it.

02

Answer on the day, because the sale is made on the day

Front Desk + CRM

Reasoning61% of every membership Bandra sells is sold the same day the enquiry arrives — 577 of 951 wins. The median wait from enquiry to purchase is 0 days. Wins that took a month or more are 20% of the volume and are worth ₹42,525 on average.

Expected impactThis is the most decisive number in the file. The studio is not running a nurture funnel; it is running a same-day counter. Response time is therefore not a service standard — it is the conversion rate, delayed. A same-day callback on every new enquiry is the highest-yield process change available and it costs nothing.

03

Fix the first-response date, then set a target against it

CRM + Front Desk

ReasoningMedian first response is 1 to 3 days across the team, and roughly a fifth of the records carry a follow-up date that precedes the enquiry itself — the field is being back-filled rather than logged at the time.

Expected impactA metric that can be written after the fact is not a metric. Until the first-response field is captured automatically, the studio cannot tell a two-hour call-back from a two-day one, and every conversation about lead handling rests on recollection rather than record.

04

Move the acquisition mix towards calls, referrals and walk-ins

Marketing

ReasoningWebsite and online sources produce 3,714 leads — 47% of everything Bandra attracts — at 8.9% conversion and ₹4,120 of lifetime value. Calls and messages produce 881 at 25.3% and ₹10,185; referrals produce 1,039 at 22.7% and ₹8,052.

Expected impactA referral lead is worth 2.5 times a website lead and converts 2.8 times as often. The studio does not need more leads; it needs the leads that already know someone. A visible referral reward, published at the point of enquiry, is the cheapest mix change in this report.

05

Decide what hosted classes are for

Programming + Marketing

ReasoningHosted classes generate 3,090 leads, and 25% of them complete a trial — the highest trial rate of any source in the file. But only 8.1% become members, at ₹3,955 of lifetime value.

Expected impactHosted classes are an excellent trial machine and a poor acquisition channel. Either the studio accepts them as community marketing and stops counting them as a growth channel, or it changes what happens at the end of a hosted class — a booked next class rather than a card left on a table.

06

Publish conversion by associate and manage the range

Studio Manager

ReasoningConversion ranges from 9.7% for Deesha Changwani to 20.4% for Nadiya Shaikh on the same product list at the same prices. Mean lifetime value per lead ranges from ₹2,655 to ₹6,804.

Expected impactThe strongest closer is not necessarily the one holding the biggest book, which is exactly why the range is worth publishing. A monthly table, without names attached, moves the middle of the distribution; pairing the top performer with the largest in-box moves the top.

07

Close the open file honestly

CRM

Reasoning2,120 Bandra leads are still marked open and their mean age is 603 days. 1,836 of them are more than six months old, and 1,076 are sitting at the same stage — pricing and schedule shared, decision never recorded.

Expected impactAn open lead that is two years old is not a pipeline; it is a missing status. A single afternoon of honest disposition — contactable, interested, or closed — would turn 2,120 rows into a short, real list and make every conversion figure the studio reports trustworthy again.

08

Work the fresh open leads and stop counting the rest

CRM + Front Desk

ReasoningOnly 56 open leads are less than thirty days old. Those are the ones the studio can still win, and they are a list one person could clear in a week.

Expected impactSeparating the file into a live queue and an archive is a five-minute change with a disproportionate payoff: it makes the daily follow-up task finite, and a finite task gets done. The archive should still be contacted at renewal season, but it should not sit in the working list.

09

Target the trial at the class people already name

Programming + Marketing

ReasoningBarre 57 is named on 5,508 leads and PowerCycle on 2,014. Between them they account for 96% of all leads with a recorded preference.

Expected impactThe studio records what each lead wants and then books the trial into whatever slot is free. Matching the first class to the stated preference is a scheduling decision, not a marketing spend, and it removes the commonest reason a first impression fails.

10

Stop buying outdoor acquisition until it can be measured

Marketing

ReasoningOutdoor classes produce 45 leads at 2% trial completion, 0.0% conversion and ₹0 of lifetime value — the weakest result in the file on every measure.

Expected impactEvery channel in this report should be held to the same two numbers: what it costs and what a lead from it is worth over a lifetime. Outdoor is currently the only line where those two are not compared, and on the evidence it is the first budget the studio should question.

11

Give the small, high-value sources more room

Marketing

ReasoningIncoming calls produce 120 leads at 27.5% conversion and ₹20,201 of lifetime value. Influencer sign-ups produce 129 leads at 14.0% and ₹10,451.

Expected impactNeither is a volume channel and neither needs to be. Both return more per lead than anything the studio spends against at scale, and both are currently incidental rather than funded. A published phone number and two more influencer partnerships are cheap experiments with a proven tail.

12

Report leads weekly, not annually

Management

ReasoningThe studio’s lead reporting is retrospective. A weekly page carrying four numbers — leads created, trials booked, trials completed and memberships sold — would have shown the September weakness in its first week rather than in this report.

Expected impactLeads are the earliest signal the studio has: they move a full quarter before attendance does. A short weekly discipline on those four numbers turns this report from an explanation into an early warning, and it needs no new system — only a standing appointment to read it.

Sources and method

The CRM lead master holds 25,315 records across all centres, of which 7,822 belong to Bandra and are used throughout. A lead is counted once, at creation, and its outcome is the status the record carries at snapshot; recent cohorts are therefore understated for conversion and overstated for open. Channel, source, associate and class preference are the studio’s own fields, used as recorded.
Response time is the interval between the lead’s creation and the first of four follow-up dates. The four comment fields are populated on every record but are largely administrative placeholders, so contact quality cannot be measured from this file at all.
Lifetime value is the value the CRM carries against the lead, not an independently reconstructed figure; it is used for ranking channels against each other, which is the only comparison it supports. Cash figures are as recorded at snapshot on 18 October 2026 and are not contribution margin.
