Reach against regularity

Two different jobs, one timetable

Reach and regularity are different properties and formats have them in different proportions. PowerCycle reached 181 members at 2.27 visits each; FIT reached 104 members at 2.66 visits each.

A format with high reach and low frequency is an acquisition and sampling product; a format with low reach and high frequency is a habit. Scheduling them as though they had the same purpose is why one of them is under capacity and the other has a waiting list.

The multi-format multiplier

Variety as a retention mechanism

38% of the members who trained here in september used more than one format, and 94 made three or more visits to a single format. The two behaviours are not in conflict: variety keeps a member coming, and a favourite keeps them accountable.

The studio currently manages neither. Nothing in the booking flow uses a member’s own favourite format to hold their slot, and nothing offers the second format to the person who has been coming to the first for months. The cross-sell exists in the membership; it has never been scheduled.

What a devotee is worth

Faithfulness measured, not assumed

Of the 394 members who attended this month, 283 gave at least 60% of their visits to a single format and 21 spread themselves so widely that no format held a third of them. The faithful average 2.10 visits a month; the generalists 8.90.

Frequency, not preference, is what the studio is buying: on this month’s numbers the devoted visit 0.2 times as often as the unattached. The commercial question is whether a generalist is a devotee who has not found their room yet, or a member about to stop.

What else could explain it

Why the league table can be read the wrong way

A format comparison is a comparison of five different decisions at once. Here is which of them the table can and cannot attribute.

Supported

Capacity, not popularity

Utilisation is seats, and seats are chosen by the studio: the largest room carries PowerCycle. A format filling 35% of a 30-seat room is outperforming one filling 60% of a 12-seat room on revenue per class, and does not look like it.

Judge formats on revenue per class and revenue per attendance alongside fill.
Partly

The trainer, not the format

PowerCycle is taught by 11 different trainers this month. Where one format has many hands, its average is a mix of standards.

The instructor chapter holds the within-format comparison.
Ruled out

The hour is neutral

Formats occupy different hours almost by definition, and hour is the strongest single predictor of fill in this data set. Any format comparison that does not hold the hour constant is measuring the clock.

Read the when-each-format-is-used grid before reading the league table.
Supported

A new format looks like a failing one

2 formats ran three classes or fewer this month.

A format needs a dozen classes and two trainers before its fill means anything.

  • Format. the Class column of the sessions export, with the Studio prefix removed so that two spellings of one format are one format.
  • Utilisation. attended places over seats offered, for that format, in the month.
  • Members reached. distinct member ids with at least one check-in to that format in the month, from the check-in ledger.
  • Devotion. a member’s largest single-format share of their own visits that month.
  • Free seat. an attendance recorded against an introductory offer, a comp or no payment record.

Formats are not comparable on price: a 45-minute strength class and a 60-minute cycle class are not the same product even when the room fills identically.

A format introduced or retired inside the month will appear with a handful of classes and an extreme fill. Every table here carries the class count so those rows can be seen for what they are.

Where the check-in ledger is used, it counts who walked in; the sessions ledger counts who was booked. The two differ by the show rate in chapter 2.

The comparison is within the month. A format whose members renew is not visible here at all; that is chapter 4.

Recommendations

14 actions, ordered by the size of the value each releases
01

Rebuild PowerCycle around what actually fills

Programming + Founder

ReasoningPowerCycle holds 35% of the timetable and fills 35%. The best-filling format in the studio, Hosted Class, gets 1%.

Expected impactMoving ten per cent of PowerCycle’s classes into the hours and rooms that already fill is worth roughly 5,929 attendances a month at current prices.

02

Scale Hosted Class on purpose, and measure it

Programming + Marketing

ReasoningHosted Class fills 86% on 2 classes - the best in the studio and among the smallest programmes.

Expected impactA format at capacity cannot tell the studio anything about demand. Doubling its classes for a month is the only way to learn whether the fill was scarcity or quality.

03

Set the complimentary budget by format, not by goodwill

Studio Manager + Finance

ReasoningMat 57 Express runs 10% free of charge, against 0% at the other end, on a month-wide utilisation of 42.6%.

Expected impactA stated budget with a stated purpose converts an unmanaged subsidy into a marketing line the studio can switch off when the purpose is met.

04

Market the second format to the devoted, not to everyone

Marketing + Front Desk

Reasoning94 members made three or more visits to one format this month and 38% of members touched more than one format. Frequency concentrates in the members who already come.

Expected impactOne named class offered to a member in their fourth week of a single format is the highest-yield cross-sell the studio has, and it costs nothing but the message.

05

Try the empty day, once, before calling it unfillable

Programming

Reasoning42 format-and-day combinations had no class at all this month. The studio has never tested them.

Expected impactOne four-week trial per gap, with the same trainer and hour as the successful version, turns an assumption into a fact at the cost of one slot.

06

Decide what each format is for

Programming + Studio Manager

ReasoningPowerCycle carries 35% of the timetable at 35.0% utilisation, while Hosted Class fills 85.7%.

Expected impactRevenue leadership and efficiency leadership sit in different formats. The timetable should be set by the second and defended by the first.

07

Give the fullest format more of the week

Programming

ReasoningHosted Class runs 2 classes and fills 85.7% of them.

Expected impactScaling what already fills is the least risky capacity decision available.

08

Sell the second format deliberately

Marketing + Front Desk

Reasoning38% of members used more than one format this month and 94 made three or more visits to a single one.

Expected impactA member with two formats and a routine is the member who is still here next year. Both halves of that can be designed for.

09

Stop judging formats on fewer than twelve sessions

Programming

ReasoningThe smallest formats ran only a handful of classes this month.

Expected impactA format cannot be assessed on a handful of appearances. Either commit to a real trial or stop reporting on it.

10

Rebalance the block that carries most of the timetable

Programming + Studio Manager

ReasoningPowerCycle holds 35% of all classes at 35.0% fill.

Expected impactA single format holding a third of the week makes the studio’s utilisation a property of that format rather than of the timetable.

11

Move express formats into the peak hours

Programming

ReasoningPowerCycle Express earns the most per class at ₹7,252 while the shortest formats run mostly off-peak.

Expected impactThe peak hour is the scarcest slot in the week and a short class is the cheapest way to use part of it.

12

Match the format to the room it is given

Studio Manager

ReasoningRooms fill from 37.3% to 62.4%, a gap driven by capacity rather than by demand.

Expected impactSeveral formats can be run in more than one room. Moving the emptier ones into the smaller room raises utilisation without new members.

13

Retire or re-launch the formats under a real trial size

Programming

Reasoning12 formats appear in the month, and the smallest ones ran only a handful of classes.

Expected impactA format judged on five classes tells you nothing. Commit to a twelve-class trial or take it off the timetable and stop reporting it.

14

Watch the show rate of every format, not only the fill

Studio Manager

ReasoningThe month closed at 42.6% utilisation across 259 classes.

Expected impactFormats that fill on paper but empty at the door behave differently from formats that convert bookings into attendance.
