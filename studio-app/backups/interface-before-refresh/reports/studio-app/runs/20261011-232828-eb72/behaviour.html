Who comes, how often, and what the gaps say

The one-line version, if you read nothing else.

Attendance is not driven by how many members the studio has. It is driven by the small group who come twice a week and the large one who came once in the whole month. This month those two groups are 52 and 205 people respectively, and they are the only two numbers worth managing.

394 people came to Supreme HQ, Bandra 1,404 times in September 2026 — 3.56 visits each. 205 of them (52%) came exactly once. The tenth most frequent member came 18 times.

The top tenth of the base produced 42% of all attendance. That is the number that makes this chapter uncomfortable to read: the studio’s floor, its revenue and its atmosphere rest on a group small enough to fit in one class, and nothing in the current reporting notices when that group changes member by member.

The median gap between one visit and the next, measured across the whole ledger for this studio, is 1 days, and 85% of all gaps are a week or less. The studio has a rhythm it can build around, and a tail it currently pretends is the same thing.

How often members buy

The purchase rhythm, and where it stops

Purchases follow the same rhythm and lag it. Across the studio’s sales ledger the median gap between one purchase and the next is 14 days, and 64% of repeat purchases come within a month. 54% of everyone who has ever bought here bought exactly once.

That last number is the studio’s real acquisition problem, and it is not an acquisition problem at all. A member who buys once and then trains for six weeks has already had the reason to buy again; the studio just never asks, in the window when the asking works.

The rhythm of the month

Four weeks, and what the last one is

Inside the month the attendance line is week 1 377 → week 2 341 → week 3 302 → week 4 287 → week 5 97. Week 5 is partial (25% of the busiest), and the members per visit count moves from 2.12 to 1.31.

A fade in the final week is not fatigue; it is the class pack running out and the renewal not yet offered. Where the studio sees a soft month, the ledger sees the same twenty days of demand followed by nothing to sell.

What the intro pack does to the numbers

A returning client on the third visit, not the second

The 2-for-1 introductory pack changes how a return is counted, so the studio’s own funnel should be read on the third visit rather than the second. Of 4,218 recorded first-timers: 1,527 came after their trial (36%), 1,027 came twice more (24%), and 814 reached a third visit (19%).

The step that matters is the third visit, and the studio currently treats the second as the milestone. A member on a two-for-one has already been "retained" on their second visit while having bought nothing, which is why the retention reports and the till disagree.

The easy readings, tested

Frequency is the number everybody explains last and the one that decides most, so it deserves its own set of rejected explanations.

Definitions used in this chapter

  • Visit. one attended check-in on the door ledger. A member who came twice in a day is two visits.
  • Visits per member. attended visits in the month divided by distinct members who attended, for this studio only.
  • Gap. days between one attended visit and the member’s next, across the studio’s whole ledger rather than the month.
  • Morning / midday / evening. each member’s own most common hour falling before 12:00, between 12:00 and 17:00, or at 17:00 or later.
  • Format used. the Cleaned Class value on the check-in row, counted as distinct formats per member in the month.

The check-in ledger counts arrivals, not intentions: a booked class nobody attended is invisible here, and appears instead in chapter 2 as a lost seat.

A member who trains at two of the group’s studios appears in both, counted only for the visits recorded at this one.

The member file records post-trial visits as the system counted them at the time of export, so very recent joiners have fewer visits recorded than they have made.

Frequency is deliberately not adjusted for the length of the membership: the studio’s commercial question is how often a paying member comes, whatever product they hold.

Recommendations

14 actions, ordered by the size of the value each releases
01

Give the 205 once-only members a dated plan, not a reminder

Front Desk + Studio Manager

Reasoning205 of the 394 members who attended in September 2026 came exactly once, 52% of the base. They are not a churn statistic; they are the next month’s frequency problem.

Expected impactOne booked class with a time attached, offered before the member leaves the building, converts a visit into a habit. On this month’s numbers a tenth of that group attending twice more is 41 extra attendances.

02

Build the at-risk list from the member’s own gap, not from the studio’s

CRM

ReasoningThe studio-wide median gap between visits is 1 day, but the distribution runs from the same day to a tail beyond two months. A fixed thirty-day rule flags half the base and misses the members who always came weekly and have just stopped.

Expected impactA gap that stretches past a member’s own median is the earliest reliable warning the data contains, and it is computable from the ledger the studio already exports each day.

03

Sell the next purchase 14 days after the last

Front Desk + Finance

ReasoningThe median gap between purchases at this studio is 14 days and 64% of repeat purchases fall inside a month. Beyond that window the probability of a second purchase falls away.

Expected impactA renewal conversation scheduled to the member’s own purchase rhythm rather than the expiry date is the difference between a renewal and a re-acquisition.

04

Program the middle of the day on purpose, not by residue

Programming

Reasoning16 members take midday as their habit against 249 morning and 129 evening, and the midday group comes 1.06 times a month against 3.72 at the peak.

Expected impactOne format scheduled specifically for the middle of the day, with an off-peak price attached, converts idle mid-morning capacity rather than competing for the evening.

05

Count the third visit, not the second, as the return

Marketing + Founder

Reasoning24% of recorded first-timers came back once after their trial but only 19% reached a third visit. A 2-for-1 pack member is a returning client on their third visit, and every retention measure built on the second one is flattering the studio.

Expected impactFixing the definition changes what the team optimises for, and costs nothing but a column in the report.

06

Protect the frequent tenth as a named group

Studio Manager

Reasoning39 members produced 42% of all attendance in September 2026. A fifth of them changing habit moves the month more than any acquisition campaign on file.

Expected impactA monthly watch-list, one message when someone in it stops coming, and no discount, is the cheapest revenue protection the studio has.

07

Book the second visit before the member leaves the building

Front Desk + CRM

Reasoning52% of this month’s members came exactly once.

Expected impactThe second visit is the transition that decides the membership, and nobody is currently asking for it at the moment it can be booked.

08

Build the timetable around mornings

Programming + Studio Manager

Reasoning249 of 394 members do most of their training before noon.

Expected impactThe evening block is not a lesser audience, it is simply a smaller one — and it is currently scheduled as though it were the largest.

09

Stop expecting members to come on a schedule

Studio Manager

ReasoningThe median gap between a member’s visits is 1 day and 85% of all gaps are a week or less.

Expected impactMembers come in bursts. A rhythm-shaped plan measures them as inconsistent when they are behaving normally.

10

Sell the next purchase inside the first month

Front Desk + CRM

ReasoningThe median gap between purchases is 14 days and 64% of repeat purchases happen inside thirty days.

Expected impactBuying here is monthly. A renewal conversation belongs in the first fortnight, not the final week.

11

Read frequency as the headline metric

Management

ReasoningVisits per member in September 2026 were 3.56.

Expected impactAttendance, revenue and member counts are all downstream of it, and it is the only one of them that moves first.

12

Treat bursts as normal and plan for gaps instead

Studio Manager + Programming

Reasoning85% of the gaps between one visit and the next are a week or less and the median gap is 1 day.

Expected impactMembers train in bursts rather than on a fixed rhythm. Plans and nudges should be built around the return after a gap, not around a perfect week.

13

Recruit the top tenth as ambassadors

Marketing + Studio Manager

Reasoning39 members produced 42% of this month’s attendance.

Expected impactThe members who already behave the way the studio wants are the best source of referrals and the most credible voices in a testimonial.

14

Ask why the weakest day is weak before adding a class to it

Programming

ReasoningFriday drew 110 visits against 282 on Tuesday.

Expected impactA quiet day is sometimes a demand fact and sometimes a timetable fact. The answer decides whether the fix is a class or a campaign.
