The book that is already sold

Renewals, unspent classes and who to call
The book that is already sold. 102 paid memberships end in the next thirty days, carrying ₹14,98,507 of original value. That is the revenue the studio will either keep or replace in a month, and it is knowable today at a name-and-date level, not as a forecast.

Those ending memberships have used 13% of what they bought on average. Members who have used nearly everything are renewals in waiting; members who have used a little are a conversation the studio has not yet had, and which becomes a refund conversation if it waits for the expiry date.

Beyond the renewals, 1,953 classes sit unspent on memberships the studio has already been paid for, and 145 members have used a quarter or less of what they bought. That is the single largest block of recoverable attendance in this review.

Definitions used in this chapter

  • Everything in this chapter. is the same figure computed the same way as in the chapter that owns it. Where two chapters disagree the underlying chapter is right and this one is rounding.
  • Delivered class revenue. the sessions ledger’s Revenue column: what the studio earned by teaching, not by selling.
  • Cash. the sales ledger’s payment value on the payment date.
  • Visits per member. attended check-ins in the month divided by distinct members who attended.
  • Benchmark. the same measure for every other studio label in the same upload, for the same month, where at least 20 classes were taught.

This is a review of one studio read from its own exports. It has no market data, no competitor pricing and no cost base beyond the payroll file, so it can say what happened and what it is worth, but not whether the studio is winning.

Figures for the current month are provisional until the month’s day-end files are complete; the last day of a month is often the one that moves a total.

Cross-studio comparisons are indicative only. Rooms, capacity, price lists and how long the studio has been open all differ.

September is compared with the same calendar month in prior years. Where the festival week or the number of trading days shifts, part of the movement is the calendar and the weekly tables are shown so it can be seen.

Recommendations

13 actions, ordered by the size of the value each releases
01

Manage frequency, not headcount

Founder + Studio Manager

ReasoningVisits per member fell to 3.56 from 4.84 while the base moved to 394 from 305. The studio is serving 503 fewer visits than the same base produced a year ago.

Expected impactOne extra visit a month for a tenth of the base is worth roughly ₹46,777 of delivered revenue and moves renewal rates before it moves cash.

02

Put a target on the open file and a date on every entry

Sales + Founder

Reasoning2,120 leads are open, 61 of them arrived in the last thirty days and 1,834 are more than six months old.

Expected impactThe fresh block converts at a real rate and costs one conversation; the stale block costs a decision to close it. Both are cheaper than new leads.

03

Use the other 3 studios as the control group

Founder

ReasoningThis studio ranks 2 of 4 on utilisation in September 2026 (42.6% against a group median of 40.6%).

Expected impactAny month where the studio moves with the group is a market month; any month where it moves alone is a decision. That distinction saves the studio from reacting to weather.

04

Publish these four numbers on one page, monthly

Studio Manager

ReasoningLeads, trials, bookings against capacity and visits per member all move before the figures the studio currently watches. In September 2026 they read 189 new leads, 1,604 booked seats and 3.56 visits per member.

Expected impactA leading indicator nobody publishes is a leading indicator nobody changes.
