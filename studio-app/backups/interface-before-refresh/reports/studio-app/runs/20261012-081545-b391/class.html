The room, the booking book and what actually turned up

The one-line version, if you read nothing else.

235 booked seats went empty in september. Late cancellations account for 100% of them; the rest never cancelled, and never came. The first number is a policy question and the second is not being measured at all.

328 classes ran at Kwality House, Kemps Corner in September 2026. They offered 4,606 seats, 2,325 were booked and 2,090 were occupied — 45.4% of the room, and a show rate of 89.9% on the seats that were booked. ₹19,01,973 of class revenue was earned at ₹5,799 a class and ₹910 per attendance.

Utilisation is the only number here the studio cannot fix by selling harder. It is set by the timetable the month before, by the weather of the month, and by how many of the booked seats turn up. Revenue follows all three, which is why the class report and the sales report disagree so usefully when they are read one after the other.

A year ago the same month ran 360 classes and filled 43.3% of them. The studio taught -32 classes and moved utilisation +2.1 points. A smaller timetable with a falling fill means the studio is shrinking and not yet choosing where.

Where the missing attendances came from

The studio did not lose its members between September 2025 and September 2026. Of the 479 people who trained here last september, 147 came back this year and 327 were new to the month. What changed is frequency: the returning core made 1,225 visits last year and 1,085 this year, 140 fewer.

Those 140 missing attendances are 7% of this month\’s total attendance. Read as a demand problem it looks unsolvable; read as the same people coming every ten days instead of every seven, it is a scheduling and habit problem, and the studio owns both.

What else could explain it

The causes that are easy to miss, and the ones that are not

Utilisation is the most over-attributed number in a studio. These are the candidate explanations for this month, in order of how much of the gap they can actually carry.

Partly

Fewer classes, or fewer people?

The timetable ran 328 classes against 360 a year earlier, so 95% of last year\’s capacity was on offer. Utilisation moves on both numerator and denominator; read the seats before blaming the demand.

If seats fell and fill held, the studio cut supply; if seats rose and fill fell, it added supply ahead of demand.
Supported

The 2-for-1 pack changes what counts as a return

A member on an introductory two-for-one is a returning client on their third visit, not their second. Read on the second visit, the repeat rate of any month looks collapsed.

Applied throughout: frequency here is visits per member from the check-in ledger, which counts what happened rather than what was paid for.

Nobody was told the room was half empty

Definitions used in this chapter

  • Class. one row of the sessions export. Where a row carries a class count above one, it is read as one scheduled slot.
  • Utilisation. attended places divided by seats offered (Capacity), not by bookings.
  • Show rate. attended divided by booked.
  • Late cancellation. the LateCancelled count on the session row: a member who released the seat inside the cancellation window.
  • Empty class. a scheduled class with zero check-ins, whether or not anybody booked it.

The sessions ledger records capacity as it was configured, not as it was sold: a class capped at 12 and sold 8 has 4 seats here whether the studio meant to hold them or not.

Attendance is counted from check-ins on the door. A member who arrived and did not scan is invisible.

No revenue in this chapter is cash. It is the value of classes delivered, which is why it will not agree with the sales ledger and should not.

The year-on-year compares the same calendar month, not a rolling four-week window, so a month of differing length or a shifted festival week will show as a movement that is not one.
