Speed decides it

The clock on the enquiry, and the clock on the reply

48.8% of wins closed on the day of the enquiry and 58% within a week. Beyond that the value of a lead decays: same day ₹32,038 · 1 to 2 days ₹31,037 · 3 to 6 days ₹48,402 · 7 to 13 days ₹64,915 · 14 to 30 days ₹69,388 as lifetime value.

The obvious reading is that fast conversion is a good sign. The reading worth more is the reverse: leads who take longer are not worse prospects, they are prospects whose trial did not happen inside the week. Speed measures the studio’s diary more accurately than it measures the member’s interest.

The follow-up book

What the studio actually did with each name

The file records between zero and four follow-ups per lead. Conversion runs 0 follow-ups 22.9% → 1 follow-ups 15.9% → 2 follow-ups 17.0% → 3 follow-ups 12.0% → 4 follow-ups 16.9%, and mean lifetime value runs ₹7,962 down to ₹4,568.

This is where the studio must be careful with its own data, because the arrow points the wrong way from the obvious reading. More follow-ups does not create more wins; the associates chase the leads they believe in, and they stop writing about the ones they do not. What the table does prove is that the practice is uneven: 1,141 leads were contacted at most once, and those are the leads the studio will never know whether it could have won.

What else could explain it

Cause, in a file that is really a diary

A lead file records what the studio did, not what happened. Every conclusion here has to survive that sentence.

Supported

The trial never got booked

65% of leads on file never completed a trial, and trial-takers convert at 27 times the rate of those who did not.

The single largest lever in this chapter, and it needs no price change.
Partly

Cheap channels dilute the pool

The largest sources are not the best-converting ones, so a month where mix shifts toward volume looks like a conversion collapse.

Hold the source constant before comparing two months.
Ruled out

Associates differ in skill alone

Book size, hour coverage and the sources each associate is handed all differ. Conversion spread of 4.9 times is not a training verdict on its own.

Assign leads at random for a month and the table becomes usable.
Partly

The CRM reflects the process, not the reality

22% of leads have a follow-up logged after they were already won, and 984 open leads are more than six months old. The file is worked as a list.

Treat it as evidence of behaviour, never as a complete record of what happened.

Definitions used in this chapter

  • Lead. one row of the CRM export for this centre. A person who enquired twice appears twice unless the system merged them.
  • Won. Status = Won in the CRM. It is the studio’s own judgement, recorded at the moment the deal closed.
  • Trialled. Trial Status = Trial Completed, as recorded on the lead. A trial that happened but was not marked is missing here.
  • LTV. the lifetime value column the CRM carries on the lead, used as recorded and not recomputed from the sales ledger.
  • Conversion %. wins divided by leads in that group, on the whole file rather than the month, because a monthly cut of these groups is too thin to read.

There is no spend figure in this file, so nothing here can say whether a channel was worth its cost. It says only what a channel delivered.

A lead marked open for years is not a lead still being pursued; the CRM has no close-out date, so ageing is measured from the enquiry.

Follow-up timestamps are whatever the associate entered, which is usually the day after. Cadence found here is a floor, not a measurement.

LTV is attributed to whoever owned the lead, so a member who later buys through a different channel keeps their original attribution.

Recommendations

12 actions, ordered by the size of the value each releases
01

Make the trial the only acquisition target

Sales + Studio Manager

ReasoningLeads who completed a trial convert at 36.0% and those who did not at 7.7%. On this file that is 1,991 leads sitting on the wrong side of a diary entry.

Expected impactEvery lead conversation ends with a time and a class, not a callback. At the studio’s current trial-to-win rate, a hundred more trials is worth roughly 28 more members a year.

02

Move acquisition effort toward Incoming sms and Client Referral

Marketing + Founder

ReasoningIncoming sms converts at 40.9% with a mean LTV of ₹23,148 on 22 leads; the largest source, Website, converts at 7.0% on 1,446.

Expected impactTripling the smaller, better source costs less than the studio currently spends to fill the larger one and delivers members who stay longer.

03

Publish conversion by associate, with book size beside it

Studio Manager

ReasoningConversion runs 8.0% to 39.3% across books of 28 to 2217 leads.

Expected impactPutting the two columns together on one page every month ends the argument about whose leads are worse and starts the one about what each person does with theirs.

04

Report leads weekly, on the stage they are stuck at

Founder + Sales

Reasoning1,141 leads were contacted at most once, and the file shows follow-ups spanning a median of 7 days for decisions that usually take one.

Expected impactA weekly thirty-minute review of the stuck stages is the only cadence that matches how quickly a lead goes cold.

05

Answer every enquiry the same day

Front Desk + CRM

Reasoning49% of memberships are sold on the day of the enquiry.

Expected impactResponse time is the conversion rate, delayed. It is also the one variable in lead handling the studio controls completely.

06

Move acquisition towards the channels that convert

Marketing

ReasoningReferrals & Word-of-Mouth converts 34.7% of its leads; Website & Online Sources converts 9.5%.

Expected impactThe studio does not need more leads, it needs the leads that already know someone. A published phone number and a referral reward are the cheapest mix changes available.

07

Close the open file honestly

CRM

Reasoning1,068 leads are open at a mean age of 782 days, 984 of them older than six months.

Expected impactA single pass to contactable, interested or closed turns thousands of rows into a short real list and makes every conversion figure trustworthy.

08

Move a share of acquisition spend towards referrals

Marketing

ReasoningReferrals convert 38.3% of their leads at ₹8,639 of lifetime value; the website converts 7.0% at ₹2,646.

Expected impactThe cheapest mix change available is asking members who already refer well to do it more often. It needs a process, not a budget.

09

Put a named owner on the largest source

Front Desk + CRM

ReasoningWebsite supplies 1,446 leads and converts 7.0%, the weakest rate of any large channel.

Expected impactThe largest pool is not the best one. Improving the biggest channel by two points is worth more than opening a new one.

10

Score associates on trials booked, not on leads held

Studio Manager

ReasoningAssociate conversion runs from 8.0% to 39.3% on comparable lead volumes.

Expected impactTrials are the step the studio controls. Leads are demand; trials are work.

11

Work the open file from the freshest stage backwards

CRM

Reasoning832 leads sit at the pricing stage and 125 at first contact.

Expected impactPricing-stage leads have already shown interest and need a decision, not a reminder sequence. They are the highest-yield calls on the list.

12

Match the enquiry to the class the lead asked about

Front Desk

ReasoningBarre 57 converts 17.6% against 17.6% for the next best group.

Expected impactWhere a lead names a class, the trial booking should be that class. It is the cheapest personalisation available and it needs no system.
