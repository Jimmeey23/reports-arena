Format comparisons

10 formats, one timetable and one audience. The format that carries 35% of the week fills 35.0% of its seats; the format that carries 15% fills 71.0%. This report compares them on eight measures at once and finds that the studio is not short of demand — it is short of the right demand in the right room.

Start here

On revenue, PowerCycle leads. On every measure of efficiency, it is last among the formats that matter.

September 2026 ran 259 classes across 12 formats, drew 1,404 attendances and attributed ₹13,06,451 of revenue to them. But the three largest formats are almost identical in scale and wildly different in behaviour. PowerCycle and Barre 57 together carry 55% of the timetable and fill 35.0% and 44.6% respectively. Studio FIT carries 15% and fills 71.0%.

The reason this is not simply a scheduling problem is visible in the member data. PowerCycle reaches 181 distinct members in the month — more than any other format — while FIT reaches 104. But of FIT’s members, 23.1% came four or more times inside the month. Of PowerCycle’s, 17.1% did. FIT is not a bigger product. It is a stickier one, and the studio is giving the stickier product less than half the week.

The league table

Eight measures, and no format wins all of them. The pattern of who wins what is the finding.

Set the formats side by side and a division of labour emerges that the studio is not currently managing deliberately. FIT wins on fill (71.0%), revenue per class (₹6,999) and regularity (23.1%). Mat 57 is second on fill at 55.1% on a small timetable. PowerCycle leads on absolute attendance simply because it runs 90 sessions. And the express formats — PowerCycle X at 52.6%, Cardio Barre X at 15.3% — are the widest spread in the table, which tells the studio that format length matters more than format family.

Read on revenue per head, however, the differences almost vanish: ₹858 to ₹1,142, a spread of 33%. That is a striking result. It means the audience is worth roughly the same per person wherever they sit. Formats do not differ in the value of the member. They differ in how many members the studio can get into the room.

Reach against regularity

PowerCycle brings the most people through the door. FIT is the only format that turns them into regulars.

Reach and regularity are different questions, and the studio currently answers only the first. In September, PowerCycle touched 181 individual members, Barre 57 180 and FIT 104. But a member who comes once counts the same as a member who comes daily. Measured by the share of a format’s audience that attended at least four times in the month, the order reverses: FIT 23.1%, PowerCycle 17.1%, Barre 57 10.0%, and the smaller formats fall to 4.8% and below.

The average member of each format tells the same story. A FIT attendee came 2.66 times in the month; a Barre 57 attendee 1.84; a Mat 57 attendee 1.55. Meanwhile 64% of Mat 57’s attendees and 61% of Cardio Barre’s came exactly once in the month.

The multi-format multiplier

Nothing in this dataset separates members as cleanly as how many formats they have used.

The lapse ledger records every format each member has trained in. Sort members by that count and the economics move in a straight line. A member who has used one format has paid an average of ₹3,307 and completed 1.7 classes. At two formats that becomes ₹8,322 and 4.6. At three, ₹15,687 and 8.3. By five formats the same member has paid ₹18,497 and completed 13.9 classes.

This is a correlation and it would be wrong to read it as a mechanism — members who were going to stay longer naturally tried more. But it is a remarkably clean gradient, and it points at a specific gap: only 38.7% of members have ever used three or more formats. Most of the book has only ever known one room.

What a devotee is worth

A member’s favourite format predicts their value better than anything except their attendance.

Members whose favourite format is FIT have paid an average of ₹22,621, completed 21.2 classes, and were last in the studio 245 days ago. Members whose favourite is PowerCycle have paid ₹10,428, completed 8.7 classes and were last seen 373 days ago — and there are 861 of them against 163. The formats with the largest followings are not the formats with the most committed ones.

When each format is used

Every format owns a day. None of them owns the whole week, and the studio schedules as though they do.

Mapping attendance by format and day shows how concentrated each product’s audience is. Back Body Blaze draws most on Wednesday, Barre 57 draws most on Saturday, Cardio Barre draws most on Wednesday, Cardio Barre X draws most on Friday, FIT draws most on Saturday. The weakest day for each is typically less than half the strongest.

Where the free seats sit

Complimentary attendance is concentrated, and it lands on the formats with the most loyal paying audiences.

Across the studio 225 attendances were complimentary in September. They are not evenly spread. Barre 57 ran 27.2% of its attendance on comps, with Mat 57 at 14.0% and PowerCycle at 15.6%, against FIT at 4.0%. At the studio’s revenue per head of ₹931, those 225 seats represent ₹2,09,367 of delivered value.

Recommendations

Nine actions, ordered by the size of the opportunity each addresses
01

Rebuild PowerCycle around the room it actually fills

Programming + Studio Manager

ReasoningPowerCycle carries 35% of the timetable and fills 35.0% of its seats, against Studio FIT at 71.0% on 15% of the timetable. Its express variant fills 52.6% in roughly half the room.

Expected impactCutting the average PowerCycle class to eleven seats removes about 180 seat-slots a month that nobody is buying, and lifts the studio-wide utilisation rate on its own.

02

Scale Studio FIT deliberately

Programming

ReasoningFIT is the best-performer on every efficiency measure: 71.0% fill, ₹6,999 revenue per class, and 23.1% of its attendees came four or more times inside the month — the highest regularity of any format.

Expected impactAdding three FIT sessions a week at the current average of 7.1 attendees, at ₹985 a head, is worth about ₹90,283 of class revenue a month from capacity the studio already owns.

03

Treat the express formats as a distinct product, not a short version

Programming + Marketing

ReasoningPowerCycle Express fills 52.6% against its parent at 35.0%, and Cardio Barre fills 27.6% against Cardio Barre Express at 15.3%. The express formats are not simply weaker — at their best they beat the full-length class in a smaller room.

Expected impactExpress formats cost less to deliver, fill a harder slot and suit a member who cannot commit ninety minutes. Building two or three deliberate express slots into peak commuter windows is a cheaper capacity expansion than adding full-length classes.

04

Market the multi-format ladder as the product

Marketing + Studio Manager

ReasoningMembers who have used one format have paid an average of ₹3,307 and completed 1.7 classes. Those who have used three formats have paid ₹15,687 and completed 8.3. At five formats the figure is ₹18,497 on 13.9 classes.

Expected impactOnly 38.7% of members have used three or more formats. Moving one member in ten up one rung of the ladder is the single largest value lever in this report, and it needs a product and a script rather than a discount.

05

Fix the formats nobody is asked to try

Programming + Front Desk

ReasoningStudio Mat 57 reaches 83 members at 55.1% fill with only 4.8% returning four or more times. Cardio Barre reaches 51 at 27.6% with 2.0% regulars.

Expected impactThese are good products with almost no habit attached. A single well-chosen pairing — Mat 57 as the recovery companion to Barre 57, Cardio Barre as the first step toward Barre 57 — turns two under-used rooms into a route rather than a stop.

06

Set the comp budget by format, not in total

Studio Manager + Finance

ReasoningComplimentary seats run at 27.2% of Barre 57 attendance against 4.0% for FIT. Across the studio that is 225 free seats, worth ₹2,09,367 at the studio’s own revenue per head.

Expected impactA per-format comp allowance makes an invisible number visible. It also stops the formats with the most loyal paying audiences from quietly absorbing the markdowns that belong to the studio as a whole.

07

Use the day grid to re-time, not to cut

Programming

ReasoningEvery major format has at least one day it owns: Back Body Blaze peaks on Wed, Barre 57 peaks on Sat, Cardio Barre peaks on Wed, Cardio Barre X peaks on Fri. The same formats are weak on their worst day by a wide margin.

Expected impactThe cheapest utilisation gain available is to move sessions from a format’s weakest day to its strongest, and from a format that is weak at that hour to one that is strong. That is a scheduling exercise, not a product decision, and it costs nothing to trial for a month.

08

Report formats by their member economics, not their revenue

Management

ReasoningRanked by revenue the formats read PowerCycle, Barre 57, FIT. Ranked by fill they read FIT, Mat 57, PowerCycle X. Ranked by members reached they read PowerCycle 181, Barre 57 180, FIT 104. Ranked by how many of those members become regulars they read FIT first by a distance.

Expected impactA single monthly page carrying utilisation, members reached, regularity and revenue per head gives the studio four views of the same product. The current revenue-only view systematically favours the format with the most sessions.

09

Watch the formats whose devotees drift

CRM + Programming

ReasoningMembers whose favourite format is PowerCycle have been absent an average of 373 days; for FIT devotees it is 245 days. The value of a FIT devotee is ₹22,621 against ₹10,428 for PowerCycle, on 21.2 against 8.7 classes.

Expected impactFavourite format is a useful early-warning field the studio already collects and does not read. A monthly count of members whose favourite format has gone quiet is a churn watch-list that costs nothing to produce.

10

Judge a format on twelve sessions or not at all

Programming

Reasoning7 of the 12 formats Bandra ran in September appeared fewer than twelve times — between them 36 sessions out of 259. The express formats ran 12 sessions in the month between them, filling 15% to 53%.

Expected impactA format that runs six times a month cannot be judged on fill rate, and it cannot be judged on revenue either. Either give a new format a real commitment — a fixed weekly slot for a full quarter — or stop reporting on it. The studio is currently making programming decisions about formats it has barely tested.

11

Let the length of a class decide, not the family it belongs to

Programming + Studio Manager

ReasoningInside the same format family the results separate sharply. PowerCycle Express fills 53% across 6 sessions; Cardio Barre Express fills 15% across 6. One is a premium express product, the other is an afternoon of empty seats.

Expected impactThe studio’s own timetable already contains the answer to its utilisation problem, in miniature: a short, specific, well-named class sells, and an unspecified short version of a long class does not. Every express format should be rebuilt to the PowerCycle Express template — its own name, its own benefit, its own slot — before any of them is withdrawn.

12

Put revenue per class next to fill rate in every programming review

Studio Manager + Finance

ReasoningAcross September the correlation between a format’s fill rate and its revenue per class is almost total — FIT fills 71% and earns ₹6,999 a class; PowerCycle fills 35% and earns ₹4,363. The two columns rank the formats the same way.

Expected impactFill rate flatters formats with small rooms and penalises formats that were given the big ones, so any review that reads fill alone will keep making the same mistake. Revenue per class is the number that survives the comparison, and pairing it with the studio’s cost per session turns a programming debate into an arithmetic one.
