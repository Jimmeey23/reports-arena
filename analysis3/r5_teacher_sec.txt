
<h2>The controlled test: same slot, different trainer</h2>
<div class="sub">This is the comparison the studio has never been able to make &mdash; and it settles a question the scorecard cannot.</div>

<p>A raw utilisation table compares trainers who teach different things at different times, which is not a fair test. So take only the slots where
<strong>the same class runs on the same day at the same time under more than one trainer</strong>, and each trainer has taught that slot at least three times
during 2026. There are <strong>{CS_N} such slots</strong>. Everything about them is identical except who is standing at the front.</p>

<p>The result is quieter than the scorecard implies, and considerably more useful. Across those {CS_N} matched slots the better trainer averages
<strong>{CS_BEST:.1f} attendees against {CS_WORST:.1f} for the weaker one</strong>, a mean gap of {CS_BEST-CS_WORST:.1f} &mdash; but the
<strong>median gap within a slot is only {CS_MED:.1f} people</strong>, and in just {CS_BIG} of the {CS_N} slots ({CS_PCT:.0f}%) does the gap exceed four.
A few slots do separate sharply, and those are real: at Monday 19:30 PowerCycle one trainer averages 8.8 attendees against 3.6, and at Wednesday 19:15 Barre 57,
6.6 against 1.3.</p>

<div class="call"><h4>What this means for how the studio manages trainers</h4>
<p>Most of the utilisation spread in the scorecard belongs to the slots, not the people. A trainer who holds the early-morning and Wednesday positions will look
weak whatever they do; one who holds Saturday mornings will look strong. Assessment should be made against the same slot, and only the handful of genuine
outliers should drive staffing decisions &mdash; in both directions.</p></div>

<h2>Where the teaching money goes</h2>
<div class="sub">The studio pays the most per session for the discipline that fills the fewest seats.</div>

<p>The payroll file separates teaching into three disciplines, and read against the timetable the pattern is uncomfortable:
<strong>Cycle sessions are paid an average of {R(_CYR)} per session across {int(_DSUM["Cycle Sessions"]):,} sessions</strong> and run at
<strong>{_CFILL:.1f}% capacity utilisation</strong>, while Barre sessions are paid {R(_BYR)} across {int(_DSUM["Barre Sessions"]):,} sessions and run at
{_BFILL:.1f}%. Cycle carries {_CSH:.0f}% of all recorded teaching sessions.</p>

<p>This is not a criticism of any individual. It is what a timetable built on programme mix rather than measured format economics produces, and it is the same
finding as the room-size analysis in the class report &mdash; seen from the cost side rather than the revenue side.</p>
